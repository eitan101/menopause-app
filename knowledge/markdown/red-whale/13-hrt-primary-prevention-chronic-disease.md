# HRT for primary prevention of chronic disease?

> **Layer 1 Source Reference:** [`HRT for primary prevention of chronic disease 13.docx`](../../sources/red-whale/HRT%20for%20primary%20prevention%20of%20chronic%20disease%2013.docx)
> **Collection:** Red Whale Menopause Guidelines | **Original Format:** `DOCX` | **Review Date:** `Unknown`

Around 25% of menopausal-age women are completely asymptomatic and do not require HRT to treat vasomotor or other hormone-related issues. However, these women may be influenced by media messages or information which suggests that HRT could be used to prevent certain chronic diseases (such as dementia, osteoporosis and cardiovascular disease), especially if started early in the menopause transition.

Women may then seek medical advice about whether they should take HRT to reduce their risk of developing health problems in later life, especially if they have first-hand experience of supporting a relative with a chronic disease. HRT has risks and benefits, and the decision to take HRT is an individual one shared between a woman and her HCP. So, should we ever agree to prescribe HRT to prevent disease?

In 2022, the US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) commissioned an updated evidence report and systematic review examining the role of HRT for primary disease prevention ([JAMA 2022;328(17):1740](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.18625)).

## Should HRT be used for chronic disease prevention?

The US Preventive Services Task Force concluded that ([JAMA 2022,328(17):1747](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.18324)):

- HRT should not be used solely for chronic disease prevention in postmenopausal women.
- HRT for disease prevention is associated with some benefits, but also with increased risk of harms.
- There was no evidence that starting HRT early in menopause reduces the risk of cardiovascular disease, dementia or death compared with later initiation of HRT (the ‘timing hypothesis’).
- Data from the WHI study found that HRT is not associated with increased risks of all-cause, cardiovascular or cancer mortality over an 18y follow-up.

### What is the British Menopause Society view?

The BMS says that:

- Oestrogen may have a protective effect in CHD prevention if initiated in women <60y or within 10y of onset of menopause (*but it does not explicitly recommend it for primary prevention*).
- HRT is the treatment of choice for the prevention of osteoporosis in younger menopausal women who are symptomatic with their menopause, and it may be considered for osteoporosis in older women (>60y) if they have ongoing menopausal symptoms as well.
- HRT should not be initiated to reduce the risk of dementia in postmenopausal women.
- Women with premature menopause should use HRT to:
  - Reduce the risk of cardiovascular disease.
  - Prevent osteoporosis.

([British Menopause Society - BMS Consensus Statements](https://thebms.org.uk/publications/consensus-statements/) – 2020 recommendation on hormone replacement therapy; primary prevention of CHD; prevention and treatment of osteoporosis).

## What data did the US Preventive Services Task Force base its conclusions on?

- The systematic review looked at 22 randomised control trials (n = 39145) and 3 cohort studies (n= 1155410) which examined the use/harms of HRT in the context of primary prevention of chronic disease.
- Trial participants were multinational, aged 53–75y, and included symptomatic and asymptomatic menopausal women.
- Results were presented as risk of disease event per 10 000 persons in users of HRT compared with risk of that disease occurring in non-users.
- Risk estimates were based on meta-analyses of at least 3 trials or, if meta-analyses were not feasible, based on results from the biggest and most reliable trial (usually the Women’s Health Initiative (WHI)).

([JAMA 2022,328(17):1747](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.18324))

## USPSTF summary of benefits and harms of HRT if given for primary prevention of chronic disease

The significant findings of the USPSF review are summarised below. Before looking at the numbers, it is important to note:

- We can NOT compare rates between oestrogen-only and combined HRT users as they are over different durations.
- The beneficial associations and increased risks lost statistical significance after stopping hormone therapy.
- The authors point out that most of the data was derived from the WHI trial. This included mainly older postmenopausal women (88% of participants were over >54y), and only looked at one HRT formulation: oral conjugated equine oestrogens with or without medroxyprogesterone acetate.

## Key Clinical Summary

> **HRT for primary prevention of chronic disease?**
> No!
> Around 25% of menopausal women are asymptomatic, but they may ask about the benefits of HRT in prevention of cardiovascular disease, osteoporosis and dementia.
> A 2022 USPSTF systematic review of the available evidence found that use of HRT for disease prevention was associated with some benefits, but also with increased risk of harms.
> The study found that oestrogen-only HRT is associated with reduced risk of fracture and diabetes, but increased risk of CVA, VTE, gallbladder disease and incontinence.
> The study found that combined HRT is associated with reduced risk of fracture, diabetes and colorectal cancer, but increased risk of breast cancer, CVA, VTE, gallbladder disease, dementia and incontinence.
> The USPSTF concluded that the evidence to date does NOT support HRT solely for chronic disease prevention in postmenopausal women.
> This viewpoint is broadly supported by the British Menopause Society.
