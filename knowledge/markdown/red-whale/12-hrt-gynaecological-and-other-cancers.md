# HRT and risk of gynaecological and other cancers

> **Layer 1 Source Reference:** [`HRT and risk of gynaecological and other cancers 12.docx`](../../sources/red-whale/HRT%20and%20risk%20of%20gynaecological%20and%20other%20cancers%2012.docx)
> **Collection:** Red Whale Menopause Guidelines | **Original Format:** `DOCX` | **Review Date:** `June 2025`

When it comes to HRT and risk of cancer, we tend to focus on breast cancer as this is the commonest cancer in women, there is a clear link with hormone use, and there has been a lot of media focus on this risk. But all hormone-sensitive tissue has the potential to be impacted by exogenous hormones, and we look here at the available information about risk of other cancers in HRT use.

*This article was updated in June 2025.*

The main references used are:

- NICE guidance on menopause: identification and management, [NG23 2015](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23), updated November 2024.
- British Gynaecological Cancer Society (BGCS) ovarian, tubal and primary peritoneal cancer guidelines: recommendations for practice update 2024 ([European Journal of Obstetrics and Gynaecology and Reproductive Biology 2024;300:69](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejogrb.2024.06.025).)

Other references are included within the article where quoted.

*Of note, NICE comments that this data can be extrapolated for those people female at birth who identify as trans men or non-binary, and who have not had gender-affirming hormone therapy*.

## Communicating cancer risk in consultations: the headlines

In essence, we can tell our patients that evidence shows that:

> Risk of ovarian cancer may be slightly increased by HRT.
> Risk of endometrial cancer may be slightly increased by sequential HRT but reduced by continuous combined HRT.
> Risk of colorectal cancer may be reduced by HRT.
> Risk of cervical cancer appears unaffected by HRT use.
> HRT does not increase the risk of death by any cause.
> Ever-use of any IUS is associated with reduced risk of endometrial, cervical and ovarian cancer. There is a possible but not statistically significant increased risk of breast cancer ([BJGP 2025; 75 (suppl 1): bjgp25X742329](https://bjgp.org/content/75/suppl_1/bjgp25X742329)).

However, it is ALSO important to remember that:

- Cancer is a multifactorial disease of which hormone use represents a small component.
- Data may also be impacted by other confounding factors which can skew findings.
- Hormones used in studies may not be the same as the therapy a patient is being prescribed.

## HRT and ovarian cancer

In summary, HRT slightly increases ovarian cancer risk.

Read on for more detail…

Epidemiological data suggests that synthetic oestrogen in combined hormonal contraception reduces the risk of ovarian cancer, presumably through its ovarian suppressive effect. The effect of HRT on postmenopausal ovarian tissue is poorly understood, and evidence looking at the effect of HRT on ovarian cancer risk is conflicting. However, numerous observational studies and meta-analyses of this data indicate a small but significant increased risk of ovarian cancer with HRT use.

- The BGCS guidance quotes mixed evidence on the impact of HRT, reflecting the different types of ovarian cancer, different types of HRT and multifactorial causes of ovarian cancers.
- NICE looked at the ovarian cancer risk of HRT in its menopause: identification and management guideline ([NICE 2024, NG23](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23)). See figures below.
- An umbrella review commented that there appeared to be a more significant association between increased incidence of ovarian cancer and use of oestrogen-only HRT than with use of combined preparations ([Cancers 2022;14:2708](https://doi.org/10.3390/cancers14112708)).
- Due to the epidemiological nature of the evidence, causation cannot necessarily be inferred.

In the [NG23](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23) guidance, NICE included a decision aid regarding HRT and risks of medical conditions, including breast ovarian and endometrial cancers, to be used in discussion with patients where appropriate (see useful resources, below). The figures it quotes for ovarian cancer risk are shown here:

| HRT use | Incidence of ovarian cancer |
| :--- | :--- |
| HRT never taken. | 1/1000 women will develop ovarian cancer. |
| HRT from age 50y for unknown duration. | 2/1000 women will develop ovarian cancer. |

Note that these figures do not apply to women experiencing premature menopause (<45y), and they do not compare the risk of combined with oestrogen-only HRT.

## HRT and cervical cancer

There is no association between cervical cancer and HRT:

- Data from the WHI found no increase in risk ([JAMA, 2003;290(13):1739](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.290.13.1739)).
- Some observational data has even shown a reduced incidence of cervical cancer in HRT users ([PLoS ONE 2016;11(1):e0147029](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0147029)).

## HRT and endometrial cancer

In summary: continuous combined HRT does not increase the risk of endometrial cancer, and may even reduce it; other regimens do slightly increase the risk.

The NICE [NG23](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23) patient decision aid quotes risks which we have summarised in the table below. Remember that these are intended by NICE for use as a *guide* for discussion with women aged over 45y requiring combined HRT.

| HRT used | Incidence of endometrial cancer |
| :--- | :--- |
| Never take HRT. | 4/1000 will develop endometrial cancer. |
| Continuous combined for unknown duration aged >50y. | 1/1000 will develop endometrial cancer. |
| Sequential HRT for unknown duration aged >50y. | 8/1000 will develop endometrial cancer. |

Read on for more detail of the evidence…

Early experience of HRT prescribing found that unopposed oestrogen therapy increases the risk of endometrial hyperplasia and cancer. Women with a uterus are therefore given a progestogen to provide endometrial protection.

### Sequential combined

These regimens administer less progestogen compared with a continuous combined preparation:

- Use of sequential regimens (especially for >5y) may be associated with a small increased risk of endometrial cancer.
- Risk is inversely proportional to the number of days a progestogen is given.

([Post Reproductive Health, 2020;26(4):181](https://doi.org/10.1177/2053369120957514))

### Continuous combined

Both the WHI and the Million Women Study showed a significantly reduced risk of endometrial cancer with continuous regimens ([Medicina, 2019;55(9):573](https://doi.org/10.3390/medicina55090573)).

Interestingly, a systematic review from 2016 found that:

- Tibolone and sequential HRT increased endometrial cancer even if used <5y.
- Continuous regimens used for >10y do not increased endometrial cancer risk and may even reduce the risk below background risk.
- Micronised progesterone appears to increase the risk of endometrial cancer regardless of regimen (although a more recent review looking specifically at micronised progesterone is more reassuring of safety ([Climacteric,2016;19(4):316](https://doi.org/10.1080/13697137.2016.1187123))).

([Maturitas, 2016;91:25](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.maturitas.2016.05.013))

## HRT and colorectal cancer

- The WHI study showed:
  - Reduced risk of colorectal cancer in the combined HRT arm but not in the oestrogen-only arm (suggesting this may be a progestogenic effect).
  - The effect was no longer significant in the long term after stopping HRT.
- Subsequent studies have supported this finding of reduced incidence of colorectal cancer among current and recent HRT user.
- There is also some evidence of reduced liver cancer in HRT users.

([Medicina, 2019;55(9):573](https://doi.org/10.3390/medicina55090573))

## HRT and overall mortality

A study examining the impact of HRT on all-cause and cause-specific mortality using data from the WHI study ([JAMA, 2017;318:927](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2017.11217)) found that:

- Over 7 years, there was no increased risk of:
  - Death.
  - Cardiovascular mortality.
  - Cancer mortality.

The authors concluded that HRT use for 5–7y does not increase or decrease risk of death from any cause, and, specifically, does not appear to impact risk of cardiovascular or cancer mortality.

## Key Clinical Summary

> **HRT and risk of gynaecological and other cancers**
> Risk of ovarian cancer may be slightly increased by HRT.
> Risk of endometrial cancer may be slightly increased by sequential HRT but reduced by continuous combined HRT.
> Risk of colorectal cancer may be reduced by HRT.
> Risk of cervical cancer appears unaffected by HRT use.
> Ever-use of IUS is associated with reduced risk of endometrial ovarian and cervical cancer, but possibly increased risk of breast cancer.
> Cancer is a multifactorial disease of which hormone use represents a small component.
> Data may also be impacted by other confounding factors which can skew findings.
> Hormones used in studies may not be the same as the therapy a patient is being prescribed.
